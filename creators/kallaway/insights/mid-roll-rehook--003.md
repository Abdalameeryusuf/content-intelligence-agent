## The pattern

A short verbal turn placed at the point where attention would naturally decay,
signalling that the most valuable information has not arrived yet. Structurally it is
always: **adversative conjunction + escalation promise + specificity marker.**

It is not a transition between topics. It is a *re-hook* — it interrupts the resolution
the viewer was about to reach and defers it.

The important observation is that this is a **structural slot, not a phrase.** The slot
recurs in every sampled video at roughly the 15–25% mark and again before the close;
the wording varies while the function does not.

## As executed here

Seven instances across five videos are listed in `source.timestamps` above. Two things
are visible from the set:

1. **Position is consistent, wording is not.** "Here's what's crazy," "here's the
   spookiest part," "but it gets even better," "but the real question is" all occupy the
   same slot.
2. **Density scales with length.** A 60-second video carries one or two. The 13-minute
   holding-company video carries almost none — the long-form uses section structure
   instead.

## Why it works

Short-form viewers are deciding whether to keep watching continuously, not once. The
re-hook arrives just before each decision and moves the payoff forward, so the decision
is deferred rather than made.

The specificity marker matters: "here's what's crazy" implies a *particular* fact
follows. A vague continuation ("there's more to it") does not create the same
expectation.

## Where it fails

- **When it does not pay off.** Used as filler it trains the audience to discount it,
  and the cost compounds across a catalog.
- **When the phrasing repeats verbatim.** Recurring exactly is how it reads as formula.
  He varies the words and keeps the slot; that ordering matters.
- **On analytical audiences.** Readers who came for reasoning experience deferral as
  padding, not as tension.
- **In long-form.** His own catalog shows he largely drops it past ~5 minutes.

## Transfer to Lifepal

**Cautiously, and probably in weakened form.** A technical audience evaluating a
personal-OS product is closer to the analytical case than the entertainment case, and
"here's what's crazy" over a Firestore export would read as mismatched register.

What transfers is the *structural* insight rather than the device: identify where a
reader will decide whether to continue, and make sure something genuinely load-bearing
sits immediately after that point. In writing, that is usually a concrete number or a
reversal, not a verbal cue.
