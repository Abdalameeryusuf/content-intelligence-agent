## The pattern

State the outcome and the time cost in the first sentence, declare the structure of
what follows, then **name the audience's impatience out loud and act on it immediately.**

Saying it aloud is the distinctive part. It converts a norm violation the audience
expects into a shared joke, and buys credit for the rest of the video.

## As executed here

- 0:00 — outcome and cost first: "in today's video I'm going to show you how to make
  your own AI chatbot in two minutes with no coding experience required, using a
  platform called Poe"
- 0:07 — a brief channel positioning line ("I make videos to show you how to use new
  technology like AI to level up your life")
- 0:17 — the structure declared: "we're going to break this video down into two parts.
  The first is actually how to make the bot, and the second is why I think this platform
  is a game changer"
- 0:26 — the naming: **"and I hate when YouTubers don't just get right into the
  tutorial, so let's do it"**

Total elapsed before the tutorial begins: 28 seconds, including the channel pitch.

## Why it works

Tutorial viewers arrive with a task, and every second before it starts is friction they
are actively measuring. Naming that impatience signals you share their priorities,
which is a cheap and durable trust signal.

Declaring the two-part structure does separate work: it tells viewers who only want the
tutorial that they may leave at the halfway point, which paradoxically keeps them —
they know the commitment has a defined end.

## Where it fails

- **When the video then has preamble anyway.** Saying it and violating it is worse than
  never saying it.
- **When the task genuinely requires context.** Some things cannot be done without
  understanding why; rushing produces viewers who complete the steps and learn nothing.
- **When overused as a mannerism.** Said in every video it becomes the preamble.
- **In non-tutorial formats.** An essay or an analysis has no "get to it" the way a
  tutorial does.

## Transfer to Lifepal

**Directly, and it fits the audience.** Developers and technical builders are the
audience most likely to abandon a piece over preamble, and most likely to notice being
respected on that dimension.

For written content the equivalent is structural rather than verbal: the first sentence
carries the finding, not the setup. "I exported my own app's database and found three
features with zero records" is the opening line; the context comes after.

[RECOMMENDATION] Adopt the underlying rule — outcome first, structure declared, context
after — and skip the verbal tic. The tic is Kallaway's voice, and copying it is the
imitation failure SKILL.md §9 warns about.
