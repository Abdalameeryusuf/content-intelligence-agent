## Claim

> "I'm starting with TikTok, YouTube and Reels because those are algorithmically
> driven, meaning they will surface my content to people that don't follow me." — 7:48

> "The problem with social media when you're starting at zero is that you have zero
> people to put your content in front of. It's very hard to grow. That's why it's
> really hard to grow audio podcasts — nobody is given podcasts to discover via the
> audio feed, so no net new people will find them just by you posting. You have to
> drive people to it." — 7:58

## Their reasoning

Platform choice at zero followers is determined by one property: whether the platform
distributes to non-followers by default. Platforms that only distribute to existing
subscribers cannot solve a cold start, because there is nothing to distribute to.

The podcast example is the load-bearing one — he uses it to show the distinction is
about discovery mechanics, not content quality or format popularity.

## Evidence they offered

**None beyond the mechanism itself.** The podcast claim is asserted from how the feed
works rather than from data on podcast growth rates.

## Scope / conditions

Explicitly scoped to starting from zero. He does not claim algorithmic platforms remain
the right choice at scale.

## Agent notes

[INFERENCE] This is a mechanism claim rather than a taste claim, which makes it more
durable than most of his advice — it stays true as long as the platforms' distribution
models stay the same, and it is checkable.

[INFERENCE] It is also the most *dated* insight in the pilot. The video is from the era
when he had ~50K TikTok followers and the catalog was brand commentary; platform
distribution behaviour changes. Before acting on this, current research on how each
platform distributes to non-followers in 2026 is warranted — this is exactly the
trigger condition in SKILL.md §8.

[INFERENCE] For Lifepal there is a tension worth naming: the algorithmic platforms that
solve cold start are short-form video platforms, and a personal-OS product for people
who have watched their own systems fail may not have its audience concentrated there.
Reaching the *wrong* 200–300 quickly is a real failure mode. This deserves an explicit
decision rather than defaulting to the platform that grows fastest.
