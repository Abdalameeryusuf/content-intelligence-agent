## The pattern

Put a **real primary artifact** on screen — a document, a plan, a spreadsheet someone
actually made — and enumerate a fixed small number of observations drawn from it, in
order, each stated as a principle rather than a description.

The artifact supplies the authority. The observations supply the value. Neither works
alone.

## As executed here

- 0:00 — "this is JK Rowling's actual outline from the fifth book" — the artifact,
  named and shown, first
- 0:12 — **observation 1, hierarchy:** every chapter has a single plot and a tie to a
  prophecy. "Plots are for individual books, prophecies are for the broader series."
- 0:23 — **observation 2, focus:** columns for a handful of characters only. "There are
  over 700 characters in the full series but she never loses sight of the handful that
  matter for each book."
- 0:38 — **observation 3, economy:** "over 4,100 pages but she never wastes a single
  chapter"
- 0:48 — the closing texture detail: "she also wrote this whole thing in pen"

The final line adds no analytical value and is the most memorable thing in the video.

## Why it works

A primary artifact is not an opinion, so the format inherits credibility that
commentary has to earn. The viewer is looking at the evidence while hearing the reading
of it.

Three observations is enough to feel systematic and few enough to remember. Each is
phrased as a transferable rule, which is what separates this from simply describing
a document.

The closing detail works precisely because it is not analysis — it makes the artifact
feel like a physical object made by a person, which re-humanizes what was becoming a
lesson.

## Where it fails

- **Without a real artifact.** A recreated or illustrative version breaks the format,
  because the authority was never in the analysis.
- **When observations are descriptive rather than principled.** "She used columns" is
  not an insight; "she never loses sight of the handful that matter" is.
- **When the artifact needs domain knowledge to read.** A schema diagram or a codebase
  cannot be parsed by a general viewer in 50 seconds.
- **When the number of observations creeps.** Five or six turns it into a list.
