## What they built

A production company built on top of an existing audience, structured so that audience
reaction functions as a demand signal before any production commitment is made.

## Decisions they made

As reported by Kallaway:

1. A book club with ~3 million members, run as the audience layer
2. **Before** publicly recommending a book, secure the production rights from the
   publisher
3. Publishers grant those rights because the club drives demand for the book
4. Audience response to the recommendation becomes the demand read
5. That read de-risks which properties to produce
6. Her company writes the screenplay and sells to streaming platforms
7. She is paid at every phase

## Outcome

Sold for **$900 million**, per the video. Named outputs: *Big Little Lies*, *The Morning
Show*, *Little Fires Everywhere*.

**Sourcing caveat:** all figures and mechanics come from a 60-second video with no
citations. `confidence: medium` reflects reporting quality, not the video's clarity —
Kallaway states it confidently, but nothing here is independently verified in this
knowledge base.

## What transfers

The **ordering**, which is the whole insight: the demand signal is acquired *before* the
expensive commitment, and the audience layer is what makes the signal available.

This is the third independent instance in the pilot of one underlying principle —
alongside `kallaway--distribution-before-product--001` (audience before product) and the
holding-company sequencing. Three different subjects, three eras, same structure.

For Lifepal the mapping is direct: the waitlist is the demand signal, and it should be
read *before* the build commitments it could inform, not merely collected as a launch
list. A waitlist of 200–300 invested people can answer which of the six phases matters
most to them — but only if it is treated as an instrument rather than a number.
